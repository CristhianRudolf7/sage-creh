\section{Research question}
The unit of design is not the model but the organization of its calls: who solves,
who checks, when to delegate, and which instructions each role receives. Holding
the model fixed, we ask whether a fifth, adaptive architecture can retain the
completion achieved by the four original architectures with less coordination.
Three objectives remain distinct: completing the whole task, reducing aggregate
tokens, and shortening user-perceived waiting time. None substitutes for another.

Version 1 compared sequential, parallel, hierarchical and reflexive flows with a
single agent and a skill-loading ablation. Version 2 implements SAGE-CREH, evaluates
two development policies and reruns every control. The contribution is this
bounded implementation with reproducible evidence, not universal superiority or
the invention of selective delegation.

\section{Five architectures and the single-agent reference}
All agents are stateless calls to the same model. A different role does not imply
different trained weights. Handoffs contain visible answers, findings or unresolved
issues, never private deliberation. Every call receives the full public task;
partial observations and reduced production context are not evaluated.

\subsection{SAGE-CREH: skill-aware gated execution}
A lightweight deterministic coordinator routes using public task structure. There
is no preliminary planning call. It selects one skill and chooses either one
proposal or two complete concurrent solvers. The second solver prioritizes
constraints, dependencies and tie breakers without seeing the first proposal.
This adaptive redundancy differs from the fixed complementary division of work
in the original parallel condition.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Identify the public contract and load only its relevant skill instructions.
\item Flag structural risk for a graph with at least eight nodes and any node with
two or more predecessors, or selection with at least ten items, forbidden pairs
and prerequisites. These are fixed prior rules, not evaluation-learned thresholds.
\item Without risk, request one proposal. Finish if its schema is valid and it
reports no specific unresolved issues; otherwise request a final review.
\item With risk, request two independent proposals concurrently. If both satisfy
the contract, report no issues, and match exactly after JSON key sorting, finish
without an integrator. Agreement does not establish correctness.
\item For disagreement, invalid shape or unresolved issues, an adjudicator checks
candidates against the original data and returns the final answer.
\end{enumerate}

The gate checks key sets, types and structure. It does not solve the task, execute
SQL or inspect references or fixtures. An incorrect integer duration, for example,
can pass this gate. Verbal confidence is not treated as a truth score. The
adjudicator receives no evaluator feedback and can be wrong. There is no selective
retry until a correct answer appears.

Let $q$ be the fraction of tasks triggering two solvers, $v$ the review rate on
the direct path, and $a$ the adjudication rate on the parallel path. With no
transport failures, mean call count is
\[
\mathbb{E}[N]=1+q+(1-q)v+qa.
\]
This equation describes measured mechanisms, not predicted accuracy. Parallel
solver waiting time is approximately the maximum of their durations, not their
sum; both solvers' tokens are still added. Avoiding an integrator removes one
complete call, but is useful only if the result retains the required completion
level.

\section{Experimental design}
\subsection{Separate development and freezing}
Before replication, ten new instances were tested: two from each non-SQL family,
with seeds distinct from the original tasks. The serial policy reviewed risky
tasks after a proposal; the parallel policy used two simultaneous solvers.
Selection favored most strict successes, breaking ties by fewer tokens and then
less time. SQL contracts were tested offline; there was no new live SQL pilot.
Both candidates' results and resource use are reported separately.

The protocol is local and precedes execution; it is not an independent
preregistration. Code, prompts, limits and order were copied and hashed before each
phase. A Python-tuple versus JSON-list comparison correction was made before the
first evaluation call, without changing tasks or policy; its amendment is retained.
The architecture did not change during final replication.

\subsection{Tasks and paired comparison}
The exact twelve original problems and their fixtures were repeated twice per
condition. Each of six families contains two problems: ledgers with 18 events,
calendars with three participants, eleven-node graphs, selecting four out of twelve
items, memory with 24 events, and two SQL queries. Ten non-SQL tasks require exact
matching; each query runs against three test databases hidden from agents, with
read-only permissions.

Nine conditions were run: single agent; the four original architectures; SAGE-CREH;
hierarchy with ten skills; SAGE without delegation; and SAGE with ten skills.
This yields 216 runs, 24 per condition. Task--repetition blocks and then conditions
within each block were randomized with seed 20260914. Conditions did not execute
concurrently; at most two calls could overlap within one flow.

\subsection{Controls, limits and potential confounds}
GPT-5.6 Luna, \texttt{low} reasoning effort, Responses API, JSON-object, non-streaming
generation and a maximum of 1536 output tokens per call, including reasoning,
were held fixed. Connection and read timeouts were 10 and 60 seconds; no transport
retries; at most five calls overall and a 240-second deadline for starting another
call. That deadline does not interrupt an HTTP request already underway.

Original controls retain their prompts and ten-description catalog plus a skill
body selected by family. SAGE omits the catalog and explicitly states JSON nesting,
especially for SQL. Comparisons therefore change more than topology. Two controls
help isolate effects: \textit{SAGE without delegation} retains its proposer and
payload but always makes one call; \textit{SAGE with ten skills} retains policy and
prompts while loading every body. The original hierarchy pair is also rerun.
Maximum per-call budgets are identical, not the actual tokens consumed.

\subsection{Measurement and analysis}
Primary completion requires every field to be correct in the original JSON
envelope. A predefined secondary v2 metric wraps a raw SQL string under
\texttt{answer} into the nested \texttt{sql} field and applies the same evaluator.
No SQL text is edited and no non-SQL answer is corrected. Primary scores are never
repaired.

Tokens sum provider-reported input and output across all calls, including
supervision, critique, repeated context and observed failures. Cached input and
reasoning are already part of those totals. Monotonic wall time includes
coordination, network and traces through the final response, excluding grading.
It is neither summed overlapping HTTP durations nor a throughput benchmark.

Exploratory 95\% percentile intervals use 10000 paired task-cluster bootstrap
draws, keeping both repetitions and all conditions together. Completion, token
and time differences and token ratios are reported. Twelve distinct tasks are
not 216 independent tasks. Degenerate intervals at a ceiling do not establish
perfect reliability.

\section{Interpretation and application}
\input{takeaway.tex}
The evaluated proposal retains a path without subagents and uses redundancy only
for part of the workflow. It does not include browsing, terminal interaction,
external tools, persistent memory, write coordination or production permissions.
A deployed implementation must replace or extend the contract adapter with skill
retrieval, retrieval-error measurement and role-specific authority limits.

Architecture choice should separate solver sufficiency, risk detection,
instruction delivery and transfer costs. A fixed integrator consumes resources
even when workers agree; early exit can avoid that step, but can also accept a
shared error. More roles or different skill labels do not create statistical
independence between responses from the same model.

\section{Validity and reproducibility}
Final tasks were already known when designing SAGE: this is a development-informed
paired replication, not a blind generalization test. Fresh development seeds do
not remove knowledge of task families or prior failures. The sample is small and
synthetic, with one model, two repetitions and short contexts; ceiling effects
and provider/network fluctuations remain. RAM, complete-project success and
equal budgets per correct response are not measured.

The package contains both waves separately, development attempts, policy decision,
frozen code, protocols, tasks, evaluator references, visible per-call traces, CSV,
XLSX and bilingual LaTeX sources. It contains no credentials or private deliberation.
Credentials were loaded from an authorized local file outside the public package.
Historical measurements do not substitute for replication controls and are not
pooled with them to estimate latency.
